# MedBrains 3.33.0

**Release date:** 2026-11-13. **Licence:** GNU AGPL-3.0-only, or a commercial
licence. Versions released before 2026-11-12 were Apache 2.0, and the client
SDKs stay Apache 2.0.

The first versioned release of MedBrains, an open-source hospital OS built for
hospitals anywhere. Everything before it was built without version numbers; this page summarises what 3.33.0 contains. The source is
[`CHANGELOG.md`](https://github.com/medbrains/medbrains/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md).

## Upgrade notes

- Database migrations apply automatically when the server starts. **Take a
  backup first.**
- After upgrading, check notifications. A hospital whose email or AI settings
  name a secret in an old format is asked to re-enter it.
- Hospitals that bill a module at the counter can leave its automatic charges
  off; the daily unbilled-orders check skips those modules.
- The tables of the retired r8r automation engine are dropped. Nothing reads
  them; export them first if you want a copy.
- Production must set its own sealing key. The server no longer falls back to
  the development key outside development.
- Custom roles whose staff give or witness high-alert doses need the new
  `nurse.mar.administer_high_alert` and `nurse.mar.witness` permissions. The
  built-in nurse role holds both; doctor and pharmacist hold the witness one.

## Latest changes

### Licence

- MedBrains is dual-licensed: GNU AGPL-3.0-only, free for everyone, or a
  commercial licence for vendors who sell, host or embed it without sharing
  their changes. Any hospital, clinic or pharmacy running it for itself needs
  no commercial licence.
- Hospitals on the free licence can buy support and service levels.
- Contributions need a signed contributor licence agreement, and a trademark
  policy says how the MedBrains name and logo may be used.
- The source of every release stays public in this repository, never behind
  a login.

### Security

- A damaged, cut or mismatched key is refused when it is loaded instead of
  being used.
- A hospital decides when MedBrains staff may reach its data: access is
  explicit, scoped, time-limited and audited.
- A manager cannot grant a permission they do not hold, and administrator
  roles and actions are hidden from everyone else.
- The clinical-trial sponsor export is pseudonymised and has its own
  permission.
- Hospitals choose who may edit payment settings, and management is told of
  every change.
- The first administrator enrols an authenticator app at setup.

- Signing a clinical document asks you to confirm who you are again, and
  anyone holding a signed print can check it by its QR code.
- Live board updates reach only the hospital they belong to. A deactivated
  account or revoked device loses its live notifications.
- Secret references in a hospital's settings are limited to that hospital.
- Single sign-on links to a local account only by a verified email.
- Dependency updates clear every security alert that had a fix.
- Development passwords are out of the scripts, the k3s join token and admin
  kubeconfig are private, and the bare-metal database cluster's etcd uses TLS
  with client certificates.
- Android release builds have no plain-HTTP default server.
- Offline sign-in fails safe: after the upgrade a device refuses it until it
  has synced revocations once online.
- Every build uses its lock file, every new package is reviewed, and a new
  dependency version must be a week old before it is taken.

### Clinical

- Every clinical record type can be signed, by its registered author.
- The lab receives its order, not the patient's whole visit.
- A code-blue page is delivered, or the screen says plainly that it was not.
- The next shift can find a returning patient and reprint the card.
- The pharmacy checks Schedule X records, allergies, look-alike sound-alike
  drugs and doses end to end.
- The doctor can dictate the consultation. It is transcribed and drafted on
  the hospital's own machine, and the doctor accepts, edits or rejects each
  section before anything is saved.
- Creating an OPD visit asks for the paper file, and the doctor can start
  without it.
- Screens and printed documents use MedBrains Sans, an easy-read typeface.
- A clinical list, the access matrix or a patient's OPD history that fails to
  load says so, instead of looking empty.
- A high-alert dose can be given and witnessed only by staff holding the
  permission for it, never by any signed-in user or an administrator account.
- A bed cannot be held twice or while occupied, and restraint checks and birth
  records refuse impossible values.
- A new admission's ward nurses get access to it, or the grant fails with an
  error; it is never skipped silently.

### Billing

- Orders done but never billed are found daily and queued for the billing desk.
- Automatic charges that a clinical action cannot post are queued for
  attention instead of being lost.
- Stock used on a patient is charged, and implants used on no one are queued
  for review.
- Staff are warned before a pre-authorisation is used up or a room charge
  takes the whole bill.
- Billing, ward stock and TPA screens show a fault when loading fails, not an
  empty list.
- A cashier cannot sign off their own day close, and write-off approval is
  checked against the patient's bill.
- A journal entry must move money, and a TDS deduction goes into the quarter
  its date falls in, with a valid PAN.
- Each bank line is reconciled to one receipt, and short credits are held for
  review.
- Patient deposits and concessions are tested end to end.

### Operations

- Switching off a module also switches off its API, not only its menu.
- The OS box uses less memory on its screen.
- The OS data disk is encrypted, with every key accounted for, and extra
  machines join the main one by approval.
- The web app loads faster: charts, the editor and language packs load only
  when needed, and half as much JavaScript loads at startup.
- Every report read is bounded, so a heavy report cannot slow the wards.
- A paperless meter shows how paperless the hospital is, by department and day.
- The desktop app is the MedBrains client for any hospital, with signed
  updates.
- The OS uses Kanidm for sign-in, takes signed updates with trial boots and
  rollback, and its screen uses the MedBrains design system.
- Long text typed into a form cannot freeze the screen, and the web app's
  offline store reports an error instead of waiting forever.
- Readiness meters for OS strength, paperless working and AI clinical flows
  are measured from a checklist with evidence.

## Downloads

Every file is listed in `SHA256SUMS`, signed with Sigstore; check a file
against it before you use it. The release page lists which file is for which
device.

- **Android:** staff and patient apps, an `.apk` to install directly and an
  `.aab` for Google Play.
- **iPhone and iPad:** staff and patient apps, an `.ipa` for App Store Connect.
- **Desktop client:** Mac `.dmg`, Windows `.msi` and `.exe`, Linux
  `.AppImage`, `.deb` and `.rpm`.
- **MedBrains OS** (x86-64 for Intel and AMD, or arm64; UEFI only):

| Where it runs | File |
|---|---|
| A physical machine, from a USB stick or DVD | the installer `.iso` |
| A physical disk, written from another computer | `.raw.zst` |
| Proxmox, KVM, libvirt, OpenStack | `.qcow2` |
| VMware ESXi, vSphere, Workstation, Fusion | `.ova` (x86-64) |
| Hyper-V generation 2 | `.vhdx` (x86-64) |
| Microsoft Azure | `-azure.vhd.zst`, unpacked first (x86-64) |
| Amazon Web Services | import the `.vmdk` (x86-64) or the unpacked `.raw.zst` (arm64) as a snapshot, then register it with UEFI boot and TPM 2.0 |

Give a virtual machine a virtual TPM 2.0, or setup asks before it stores
anything unencrypted.

## Known limits

- The MedBrains OS image is a preview. Do not use it for patient data yet.
- The OS image is not signed for Secure Boot yet.
- The iOS, macOS and Windows builds are unsigned until the signing
  certificates are added. Files marked `-unsigned` will not install on iOS,
  and macOS and Windows warn before opening them.
- The OS images have not yet been tested on real VMware, Hyper-V, Proxmox, AWS
  or Azure.

## What 3.33.0 contains

- **The hospital system:** registration and UHID, OPD with token queues, IPD
  and discharge, laboratory, radiology with PACS, pharmacy with NDPS and
  schedule controls, billing with online, UPI QR and card-machine payments,
  HR, and more.
- **One case sheet per patient,** signed clinical documents, and printing with
  signatures and DRAFT marks.
- **Country integrations, India first:** ABDM M1 to M3, Scan & Share, NHCX eligibility,
  pre-auth and claims, and UHI. FHIR bundles are checked against the official
  HL7 validator before they are sent.
- **Apps:** the web app, native iPhone and Android apps for staff and
  patients, and TV boards.
- **Hospital OS foundations:** a kernel for tenant-scoped work, configurable
  discharge flows, drug-interaction and critical-value changes with two-person
  approval, and connected vendor apps with a published extension protocol and
  SDKs.
- **Security and operations:** row-level security on every table holding
  hospital data, an append-only audit of every change and PHI read, backups
  sealed to an offline key with restore drills, and signed release artefacts
  with SBOMs and checksums.
