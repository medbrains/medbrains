# MedBrains Goal 1 - Continuous Patient Flow Problem

## Core Problem

MedBrains is at risk of becoming a collection of hospital modules instead of one
continuous hospital operating system. The practical problem is that a patient can be
registered, queued, admitted, dispensed medicine, billed, reported, displayed on a
token board, or opened on mobile/TV/kiosk screens without every surface sharing the
same patient context, event state, permissions, masking rules, and operational
readiness.

When this happens, staff must re-search patients, repeat data entry, guess the next
valid action, work around disabled buttons, or trust reports and displays that are
not tied to the frontline workflow. That creates delay, billing leakage, privacy risk,
patient-safety risk, and poor adoption even when every individual module technically
exists.

## Problem To Solve

The goal is to make one patient journey stay coherent across every operational
handoff. A patient who starts at registration, OPD, emergency, camp, or IPD should
carry the same identity, encounter, admission, order, invoice, permission, masking,
and event context wherever the workflow goes next.

The product problem is not "add more module rules." The problem is to remove the
breaks that force hospital staff to manually reconnect patient context across:

- Registration to OPD, emergency, camp, or IPD.
- OPD or emergency to orders, pharmacy, admission, billing, reports, and follow-up.
- IPD admission to ward work, pharmacy, billing, discharge readiness, reports, and
  patient-facing or public displays.
- Camp screening to hospital referral, OPD/IPD/emergency follow-up, pharmacy,
  billing, and reports.
- Pharmacy and billing work that starts from patient-linked events and orders rather
  than duplicated manual context.
- Settings that explain which routes, actions, reports, token boards, printouts,
  mobile screens, TV screens, kiosk flows, permissions, and masking rules govern the
  actual patient journey.

## Staff Problems

### Front Desk

Registration staff need to identify or register a patient once, see the correct care
handoff, and avoid exposing more demographic information than the user's permission
allows.

### OPD And Emergency

Nurses and doctors need to open a patient from the right queue, understand the
current encounter, record clinical work, create orders or admission handoffs, and see
why an action is unavailable when patient state, missing events, permissions, or
regulatory requirements block it.

### IPD Ward

Ward staff need an admission workspace that keeps patient context, bed/admission
state, care tasks, orders, pharmacy handoffs, billing readiness, discharge readiness,
reports, and role-based actions visible through stable routes and tabs.

### Camp Teams

Camp staff need screening and registration workflows that preserve camp context when
the patient is referred into OPD, IPD, emergency, pharmacy, billing, reporting, or
follow-up.

### Pharmacy

Pharmacy staff need patient-linked medication work that shows formulary, schedule,
controlled-substance, batch, expiry, FEFO, allergy, and order status context before
dispensing or returning medicines.

### Billing

Billing staff need invoices, payments, payer claims, and discharge bills to come from
actual patient events, admissions, orders, and pharmacy activity rather than manual
reconstruction.

### Administrators

Administrators need settings and reports that reveal coverage gaps: which patient
journey actions are event-backed, permission-checked, masked, reportable, printable,
or available on web, mobile, TV, kiosk, and token-board surfaces.

### Public And Edge Devices

Token boards, kiosks, TV dashboards, bedside mobile views, and printouts need to show
only the minimum safe information while still reflecting the same canonical workflow
state as the staff application.

## Solved State

This problem is solved when a user can start with a patient at registration and
continue through OPD, IPD, emergency, camp, pharmacy, billing, reports, and device
surfaces without unnecessary re-searching, duplicated context entry, unsafe identity
exposure, unexplained disabled actions, or disconnected reports.

Every patient-flow slice should be chosen because it reduces one of these concrete
failure modes:

- Staff cannot tell which patient, encounter, admission, camp, order, invoice, or
  queue context an action will use.
- A handoff exists in one module but is hidden or unavailable in the next place staff
  need it.
- An action is blocked without a reason tied to patient state, event state,
  permissions, configuration, masking, or regulatory requirements.
- Reports, printouts, token boards, mobile screens, TV screens, or kiosk surfaces show
  a different truth than the staff workflow.
- Administrators cannot see which part of the patient journey is missing coverage or
  governed by the wrong access policy.

## Evidence Of Progress

Progress on this goal is measured by working reductions in those failure modes. A
completed slice should show a before-and-after patient-flow improvement, such as:

- A handoff becomes available from the patient workspace at the right time.
- A blocked action shows a traceable reason: context, event, permission,
  configuration, masking, or regulatory control.
- A report or indicator is linked to the same event that drives the operational
  workflow.
- A token board, kiosk, mobile, TV, or print surface uses the same access and masking
  policy as the web workflow.
- Settings exposes a real coverage gap and points to the affected patient journey
  action.
- A stable route or tab replaces a hidden drawer/menu for durable clinical,
  operational, or administrative work.

Verification exists to prove the hospital problem is fixed. Tests, type checks,
API-contract checks, and UI/runtime evidence should be attached to the touched
workflow only when they demonstrate that the patient-flow break no longer occurs.
