# MedBrains Goal 1 - Hospital Workflow Problem Statement

## Problem Statement

MedBrains must solve the real hospital problem of moving a patient through care
without losing context, safety controls, or operational accountability. The current
product risk is not a lack of modules. The risk is that registration, OPD, IPD,
emergency, camp, pharmacy, billing, reports, settings, mobile, TV, kiosk, token, and
print surfaces can behave like separate systems even when they are handling the same
patient.

In a working hospital, staff need one continuous patient-flow workspace. A patient is
registered or identified, routed to the right care setting, clinically assessed,
ordered for services or medicines, admitted or discharged where needed, billed,
reported, and displayed on staff or public devices with only the information safe for
that surface. The system should make the next valid action obvious from the patient
state, current encounter or admission, orders, invoices, canonical events,
permissions, masking policy, and regulatory constraints.

## Product Failure To Close

MedBrains can look feature-rich while still being hard to use if staff must re-search
the same patient, jump between unrelated pages, guess why an action is unavailable,
or trust reports and device displays that are not tied to the same events used by
frontline workflows. The goal is to close that gap by making each touched area prove a
specific patient-flow outcome.

## Hospital Situations This Goal Covers

1. Registration desk:
   A clerk registers or updates a patient, verifies identity, applies UHID and
   demographic masking expectations, and sees the valid handoff to OPD, emergency,
   camp, or IPD without reopening the patient elsewhere.

2. OPD clinic:
   A nurse or doctor opens a queued patient, sees the live patient context, records
   vitals or consultation details, creates medication, lab, radiology, referral, or
   admission handoffs, and sees blocked actions explained by patient state,
   permissions, missing events, or regulatory controls.

3. IPD ward:
   Ward staff work from an admission command workspace where patient context,
   bed/admission state, care tasks, orders, pharmacy handoffs, billing/discharge
   readiness, reports, and role-based actions are visible through stable routes and
   tabs.

4. Emergency and MLC:
   Emergency staff triage and treat patients, document MLC when applicable, transfer
   or admit, and trigger pharmacy, billing, and report events with auditability,
   privacy controls, and legal traceability.

5. Camp workflow:
   Camp teams register attendees, screen patients, refer into OPD, IPD, or emergency,
   and preserve camp context through billing, pharmacy, reports, and follow-up.

6. Pharmacy:
   Pharmacy staff receive patient-linked orders, verify formulary and regulatory
   flags, handle controlled or scheduled medicines, batch, expiry, FEFO, dispensing,
   and return events that update patient flow and billing readiness.

7. Billing:
   Billing staff create invoices, payments, discharge bills, and payer claims from
   actual patient events and orders instead of re-entered clinical context.

8. Settings and governance:
   Administrators see which permissions, masking rules, routes, reports, print
   outputs, token boards, mobile views, TV views, and kiosk flows are governed, which
   are missing coverage, and which real workflow each rule affects.

9. Mobile, TV, kiosk, token, and print surfaces:
   Bedside mobile screens, public token boards, TV dashboards, kiosks, and printouts
   reuse the same event state and access policy as web while exposing only the minimum
   safe information for their location and audience.

## Concrete Outcomes

- Patient context remains available from registration through OPD, IPD or emergency,
  camp where relevant, pharmacy, billing, reports, and settings.
- Durable clinical and operational work happens through stable routes and tabs, with
  drawers and modals limited to short transient tasks.
- Menus, tabs, buttons, table actions, handoffs, print actions, token boards, reports,
  mobile, TV, and kiosk surfaces activate from canonical events, patient state,
  configuration, and permissions.
- Unavailable actions have a traceable reason such as missing patient context, missing
  event, missing permission, masking requirement, configuration gap, or regulatory
  block.
- Settings exposes coverage and gaps for access, masking, routes, reports, print,
  token, mobile, TV, and kiosk surfaces.
- Reports and indicators use the same canonical events and payload fields that drive
  operational modules.
- Public and edge-device surfaces apply shared minimum-disclosure and masking
  behavior consistently.

## Priority Problem Areas

1. Patient context continuity across registration, OPD, IPD, emergency, camp,
   pharmacy, billing, reports, and settings.
2. Event-driven action readiness for routes, tabs, buttons, handoffs, print actions,
   token boards, reports, mobile, TV, and kiosk surfaces.
3. Permission and masking coverage for patient identity, contact, clinical,
   regulatory, operational, financial, report, print, and public-display fields.
4. IPD ward command workspace with top patient context, left navigation, central
   route/tab work areas, and right-side readiness/actions.
5. Settings coverage that exposes governed surfaces and coverage gaps instead of only
   generic permission lists.
6. Reports and indicators linked to canonical workflow events.
7. Mobile, TV, kiosk, token-board, and print parity where the device solves a real
   hospital workflow and applies the same access and event model.

## Completion Evidence

This goal is complete when the application demonstrates that staff can move a patient
through the covered hospital situations without unnecessary re-searching, context
loss, unsafe disclosure, unexplained blocked actions, or reports disconnected from
frontline events. Each completed problem area should have focused unit tests, static
or contract checks, and relevant UI or runtime evidence for the touched workflow.
