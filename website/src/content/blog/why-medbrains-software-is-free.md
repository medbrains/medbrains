Most hospital software is sold the way office software is: a licence or a
monthly subscription, often counted per user or per module. We decided not to
do that. **The MedBrains software, and every update to it, is free for every
hospital and clinic.** There is no subscription and no charge per login.

What we charge for is the work that decides whether a hospital system actually
succeeds: setting it up, shaping it to the hospital, and keeping it running.
This post explains the choice, what it changes for a hospital, and what it
costs us.

## What is free, and what is not

Free, for everyone:

- every module, from registration and OPD to IPD, lab, radiology, pharmacy, OT
  and GST billing with cashless claims;
- every update, for as long as the hospital runs MedBrains;
- the staff and patient apps, TV token boards and the kiosk;
- the clinical AI assistant, within fair use;
- every patient-safety rule, and a full export of the hospital's data whenever
  it asks.

What we charge for, each quoted in writing before the hospital commits:

- **Implementation**, once: departments, tariffs and forms configured, patients
  and masters brought across, every team trained on its own screens, and a named
  go-live lead.
- **Customisation**, per requirement: a report, form, integration or workflow
  only that hospital needs.
- **AMC**, yearly: help desk, bug fixes, monitoring, checked backups, small
  configuration changes, and hosting in India if we run the system for them.

The full list is on our [pricing page](/pricing), and the terms behind it are in
our [commercial terms](/terms).

## Why we chose this

### A licence is not what makes a hospital system work

Hospitals rarely fail with software because they could not buy it. They fail
because it was set up for someone else's workflow, the night staff were never
trained, the old data never came across, or nobody answered the phone in the
second month. In our experience, the go-live, the training and the
support afterwards decide the outcome far more than the licence does.

So we put our price on that work. When we charge for implementation, we are paid
for the part that decides the outcome, and the hospital can see exactly what it
is buying.

### No meter on logins

Per-user pricing quietly teaches a hospital to ration accounts. Staff share a
login, the audit trail records the wrong person, and a safety check that relies
on knowing who is acting becomes meaningless. With no charge per user, every
nurse, technician and clerk can have their own account, which is what patient
safety and NABH both expect.

### One version for every hospital

Because updates are free, no hospital stays on an old version to save money. A
safety fix or a regulatory change reaches the small nursing home on the same day
it reaches the large hospital. For us it also means maintaining one product,
not a ladder of editions.

### Free is not new in health IT

This is not an untested idea. Bahmni, an open-source hospital system, is free
software, and hospitals pay partners for implementation and support. The
government's e-Hospital service is offered to public hospitals without a licence
fee. We follow the same principle, with one team responsible for the software,
the implementation and the support.

## What we achieve with it

- **More hospitals on a proper record.** A smaller hospital or a trust hospital
  can start without a licence budget, paying for the work its go-live needs.
- **Our income follows the hospital's success.** AMC renews only if the hospital
  is well served, so our incentive is to keep the system running well, not to
  sell more licences.
- **Clear prices for clear work.** Each quote lists what we will do, item by
  item. There are no modules held back to sell later.
- **Charitable, trust and government hospitals** get implementation at cost.

## What we will not do

A free product has to be paid for somehow, and the usual ways are the ones we
rule out. No money from pharmaceutical companies or advertisers reaches the
product. We never sell clinical data: under India's Digital Personal Data
Protection Act we act as a processor for the hospital's patient data, working
only on the hospital's instructions. And every patient-safety feature stays free
for everyone. Safety is never an upgrade.

## The trade-offs we accept

This model has costs for us, and we would rather say so. Our income comes in
lumps, at each go-live, instead of a steady monthly subscription. And our growth
is limited by how many implementations our team can do well, so we take on
hospitals at the pace we can serve them properly rather than as fast as we can
sign them.
