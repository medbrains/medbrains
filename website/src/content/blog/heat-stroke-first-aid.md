Heat stroke happens when the body can no longer cool itself. It can damage the
brain, heart and kidneys within hours, and it can kill. The good news: it is
mostly preventable, and quick first aid makes a real difference.

## Heat exhaustion comes first

The early stage is **heat exhaustion**. Watch for:

- heavy sweating, thirst and tiredness
- headache, dizziness or feeling faint
- nausea or vomiting
- muscle cramps

**What to do:** move to a cool, shaded place, loosen clothing, sip water or oral
rehydration solution, and rest. If the person does not improve within about
30 minutes, get medical help.

## Heat stroke is an emergency

Signs that it has become **heat stroke**:

- confusion, slurred speech or strange behaviour
- fainting or not responding
- a seizure (fit)
- very high body temperature, with skin that may be hot and dry

**What to do, at once:**

1. Call for an ambulance or take the person to the nearest hospital.
2. Move them out of the sun and remove extra clothing.
3. Cool them fast — wet the skin with water and fan them, or put cold packs
   in the armpits, neck and groin.
4. Do not give anything to drink if they are confused or not fully awake.

## Who is most at risk

Older people, infants and young children, pregnant women, people working
outdoors, and people with heart, kidney or lung disease or diabetes. Check on
neighbours who live alone during a heat wave.

*This article is general health information, not medical advice. In an
emergency, go to the nearest hospital.*
